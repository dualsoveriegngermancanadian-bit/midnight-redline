# Midnight Redline — Staged Release Plan

## Part One: The Core Night Run

Part One delivers the complete playable loop: the featured showroom, selectable vehicle lineup, prepaid race access, itemized vehicle acquisition, garage parts and services, compatibility checks, dyno validation, performance-band matching, event entry, race telemetry, in-game winnings, the first progression arc, engine-out service states, and the pit-crew replacement boundary. Players can see what each part changes, build a car, test it, race it, win or lose, and allocate in-game winnings toward the operation.

Part One’s records use stable identifiers for vehicles, engines, parts, services, orders, entitlements, race events, and progression milestones. Prepaid access has an explicit covered-through date. Customer commerce and in-game winnings remain separate ledgers. The active payment boundary remains RBC business e-transfer with bank-confirmed order status; live collection is not enabled by the game itself.

## Part Two: Full Team Operation

Part Two expands the world with a complete team-owner layer: fifth-wheel hauler ownership, multiple cars, spare-engine inventory, specialist crew roles, transport and hospitality, larger parts logistics, deeper suspension and chassis tuning, reliability and heat management, additional tracks and rivals, expanded race-weekend scheduling, and national-scale paddock presentation.

Part Two adds content through new catalog and progression identifiers rather than rewriting Part One records. Existing vehicles, installed parts, covered access, dyno history, and completed events remain readable. New team assets can be introduced as optional capabilities with clear effects and prices, while Part One remains playable without requiring a reset.

## Release Discipline

Part One is complete only when the showroom-to-garage-to-dyno-to-race path is testable and every listed paid item has an effect or an explicit cosmetic label. Part Two begins after the Part One core loop is stable. Neither release may use hidden fees, unannounced loss, or automatic recurring e-transfer assumptions. Any live payment activation remains a separate, user-approved bank-side step.
