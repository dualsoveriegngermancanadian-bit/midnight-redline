# Midnight Redline — Race Economy and Moving Calendar

Race winnings are the progression engine for the team. A completed event produces an in-game result ledger that can fund parts, engine service, crew capability, transport, and hauler upgrades. Customer purchases remain a separate commercial ledger for prepaid access, vehicle products, race access, and garage items.

| Ledger | Source | Uses | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Race winnings | In-game event results and awards | Team upgrades, parts, service, spares, transport, and progression | Game funds only; never presented as a cash payout or customer receipt. |
| Customer commerce | Confirmed RBC business e-transfer orders | Paid access, vehicle acquisition, and itemized shop products | Order reference and confirmed status required before entitlement. |
| Team operating costs | Service, transport, crew, and event preparation | Reduce available in-game team funds | Shown before commitment so the player can plan. |

The race calendar advances as the player progresses. Each event has a scheduled window, rival readiness, track condition, entry requirement, and service deadline. Entering the garage does not stop the world clock; the player must decide whether to dyno, repair, swap an engine, stage another car, or accept the next matchup. A missed service window can make a car unavailable without deleting ownership or forcing an unannounced purchase.

Winning an event advances the circuit and unlocks more demanding rivals, larger in-game purses, new equipment tiers, and additional team responsibilities. A loss can consume event resources or delay progression, but it does not secretly charge a real-money payment. The player sees the consequence before confirming a race.

The team-owner loop is therefore: earn a result, allocate winnings, maintain the operation, prepare the appropriate car, validate the build, and meet the next race window. Commercial receipts and player awards are never mixed in the same balance or represented as the same kind of money.
