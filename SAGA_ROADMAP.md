# Midnight Redline — Saga Roadmap

Midnight Redline is designed as a multi-part racing saga. Each part delivers a complete playable arc while carrying forward stable vehicles, builds, team history, and earned progression. Future chapters are previews and expansions, not hidden requirements for enjoying the current release.

| Part | Working arc | Main expansion |
|---|---|---|
| Part One | The Night Run | The first Harborline qualifier, featured creator build, vehicle selection, garage upgrades, dyno validation, pit service, rival events, winnings, and the first host challenge. |
| Part Two | Team Line | Team ownership, hauler acquisition, crew recruitment, multiple cars, spare engines, pit logistics, and the move from independent racer to organized operation. |
| Part Three | City Circuit | Race-to-race travel across new cities, local rival crews, track conditions, transport planning, and regional advancement. |
| Part Four | House of Speed | Team relationships, crew roles, loyalty, specialist development, and role-play decisions that change preparation and race-weekend outcomes. |
| Part Five | Heavy Iron | Larger haulers, engine programs, advanced suspension and chassis tuning, reliability management, and deeper professional race operations. |
| Part Six | National Lights | Major competitions, multi-car staging, sponsor and hospitality operations, rival-team politics, and a national calendar that keeps moving. |
| Part Seven | The Long Haul | Cross-region logistics, championship strategy, advanced pit windows, weather, travel risk, and the consequences of earlier team decisions. |
| Part Eight | Final Host Run | The long-form payoff: the strongest rival field, the complete team operation, the final creator-host build, and the culmination of the city-to-city campaign. |

## Part One Handoff

Part One ends with the player understanding the complete core loop: choose a car, purchase and install meaningful hardware, validate the build, enter a matched event, handle wear or failure, collect in-game winnings, and advance the calendar. The closing board previews the next chapter’s team-management layer without blocking Part One’s conclusion.

## Continuity Rules

Stable identifiers preserve owned vehicles, engines, installed parts, dyno records, race results, covered access, and team reputation. Later parts add content rather than silently rewriting earlier purchases. Customer commerce remains separate from in-game winnings, and each paid item remains visible with its price and effect. The active payment boundary is RBC business e-transfer with bank-confirmed orders; no automatic recurring transfer or hidden charge is assumed.

## City and Relationship Arc Detail

Part Three introduces the travel board: each city has a track identity, rival-team culture, weather profile, transport distance, and event schedule. Part Four makes the crew personal: the player recruits and develops a crew chief, engine specialist, tuner, chassis specialist, logistics lead, and event manager whose trust and role performance affect preparation.

Part Five makes equipment and reliability central. The player chooses which cars and spare engines travel, decides whether to spend on redundancy or performance, and manages heat, wear, service time, and transport risk. Part Six turns the operation into a national team with sponsor-facing hospitality, multiple rival organizations, and a calendar that requires long-term planning.

Part Seven brings consequences from earlier choices into the long haul. Crew loyalty, equipment investment, city relationships, and race results influence which opportunities open and which rivals become recurring threats. Part Eight resolves the creator-host campaign through the strongest build, the most complete team operation, and a final competition that reflects the player’s accumulated decisions.

Every chapter remains mechanically honest: relationship or role-play choices can change preparation and opportunity, but they do not secretly alter product prices or grant paid entitlements. New cars, engines, parts, and team assets use additive identifiers so cross-part continuity remains stable.
